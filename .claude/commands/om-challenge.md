# /om-challenge

Read `syntheses/README.md` first and follow it: scope, search, reading budget and writing rules. If it is missing, stop and say so. **Never modify a file outside `syntheses/`.**

The aim is to test the thesis, not to approve it. Do not be agreeable.

1. **Read the vault's rules**: `AGENTS.md`, and `.claude/rules/local.md` if it exists.
2. **State the thesis** from `$ARGUMENTS`, or from the conversation if the argument is missing. If it stays vague, ask.
3. **Draw out its premises**: two to five sentences, each open to challenge on its own.
4. **Search the vault for counter-evidence** (README §2), keyword queries per premise and key term. Look especially at:
   - past **decisions** that contradicted or reversed a close idea (`brain/Key Decisions.md`, decision notes);
   - **failures and lessons** (`brain/Gotchas*.md`, `brain/Patterns.md`, `work/archive/`, any journal);
   - **opposing positions**: notes where the user, or an author they cite, argued the reverse or flagged the risk.
   Read the notes kept, within README §3.
5. **Write the analysis** under: **Thesis** (restated clearly); **Premises** (numbered); **Counter-arguments from the vault** (each with the premise it targets, a short quote, the `[[link]]` and its date); **Blind spots** (what the vault's history suggests was overlooked, each grounded in a `[[note]]`); **Verdict** (consistent with recorded experience, or a call for caution).
6. If nothing in the vault contradicts the thesis, say so plainly — but only after searching everywhere.
7. **File**: `syntheses/Challenge - <thesis in a few words>.md` (README §4), type `challenge`, `produced-by: om-challenge`. Record the engine, queries and notes read.
8. **Report**: the verdict in one sentence, the three strongest counter-arguments, the path written.
